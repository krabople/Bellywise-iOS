# Bellywise observational method

This is an explainable diary comparison engine, not a diagnostic model. It has not been clinically validated. Product readiness gates (14 complete days, 6 exposed and 6 unexposed days, three separate exposure runs) are conservative design choices, not validated medical thresholds. Do not market an "emerging" pattern as a confirmed intolerance, probability of intolerance, or treatment recommendation.

## Observation and missingness

The unit is a local calendar day, never a meal or an individual repeated symptom entry. For an exposed same-day observation, a symptom counts only when at least one occurrence was logged at or after the first recorded ingredient exposure that day. A symptom logged only before the food cannot support that food-to-symptom direction. An explicitly logged qualifying ingredient and symptom may contribute from an unfinished past day. Ingredient absence or symptom absence is used only when a daily check-in certifies that the day was complete and the feeling was actively tracked. Unlogged items on unfinished days remain missing, never negative outcomes. This lets real entries contribute without manufacturing symptom-free controls. Today and future days are excluded because they have not finished. Days containing an unresolved inferred custom food or a meal with no ingredients are excluded from comparisons; their unknown ingredients cannot reliably supply unexposed controls. The last check-in for a date wins, even if it reverses completion. Symptom occurrences are deduplicated within a day. Positive feelings are independently named outcomes, never substituted for absence of a negative symptom. Stronger "emerging" status and chance checks continue to require enough completed comparison days.

Each check-in snapshots `trackedSymptomIds`: the feelings selected for tracking plus any explicitly logged that day. A comparison only uses an outcome day whose snapshot includes the feeling. Selecting a new/custom symptom must not convert old untracked days into symptom-free controls. Unselected feelings can still be analysed historically from their saved snapshots. Legacy check-ins with no snapshot require explicit review and re-saving before they contribute to comparisons. Editing or deleting a food/drink or feeling invalidates the check-in for both the original and revised calendar dates.

Timezone interpretation uses the device's current local timezone. The app does not yet persist the timezone at entry, so travel or a timezone change can reassign a historical timestamp's calendar date. Severity is analysed separately from binary occurrence, as described below. Dose, biologically specific latency, ongoing symptom duration, medications, illness, menstrual phase and meal interactions are not modelled; they remain material limitations.

## Two prespecified windows

1. **Later the same day** compares ingredient presence with symptom occurrence at or after the first recorded exposure on that date. Earlier symptoms do not count as outcomes for that exposure. This ordering check cannot establish a biologically plausible latency, and approximate times or a continuing symptom can still mislead it.
2. **Following calendar day** uses ingredient presence on day D and symptom occurrence on D+1. Complete-day inference requires both dates; explicitly observed exposures and outcomes can also support exploratory observations on unfinished days. Missing dates are never skipped to find the next available day. It is a calendar window, not a fixed 24-hour delay; other meals on the symptom day may contribute.

All ingredient/outcome/window combinations with at least one exposed and one unexposed day are tested, including weak effects and sparse comparisons. Both occurrence and intensity tests enter the full correction family before choosing a display window or filtering for positive associations. One window per ingredient/outcome is displayed, ranked by readiness then observed frequency or intensity difference. A frequency finding needs at least two outcome days with the ingredient; an intensity finding needs at least three rated feeling days in each group. Negative symptoms occurring less often or with lower intensity are not promoted as evidence that a food is protective.

## Effect and uncertainty

The frequency effect is the absolute difference in occurrence proportions: `symptom days / exposed days - symptom days / unexposed days`. A 95% Newcombe score interval is constructed from Wilson limits. A two-sided Fisher exact p-value sums fixed-margin tables with probability no greater than the observed table. Benjamini–Hochberg jointly adjusts frequency and intensity p-values before display filtering. An unavailable intensity test contributes p=1 rather than disappearing from the prespecified family.

"Emerging" requires 14 eligible observations, at least 6 exposed and 6 unexposed days, at least 6 confirmed exposure days, three separate exposure runs, risk difference at least 20 percentage points, a lower interval bound greater than zero, adjusted p <= .05, no flagged stress or sleep imbalance, and a confirmed-exposure sensitivity difference at least 20 points. Otherwise comparisons are "exploratory" or "not-enough-data". These labels describe diary evidence, not clinical certainty. Confidence intervals are unadjusted and assume independent binomial observations; serial dependence of diary days, correlated foods and repeated re-analysis may make inferential measures optimistic. BH is a multiple-comparison guardrail, not a guarantee for these dependent observations.

Separate exposure runs require observed unexposed days between them; missing dates cannot manufacture a stopped-and-restarted exposure. Even correctly ordered observations do not establish causation or distinguish persistent symptoms from new onset.

## Intensity and severity

For each usable outcome day with an explicitly logged feeling, the intensity score is the highest valid 1–5 rating among qualifying entries. For same-day exposed observations, only entries at or after the first recorded ingredient exposure qualify; following-day observations use the following calendar day's ratings. Repeated entries cannot add independent observations or be summed into a larger severity score. Missing/invalid ratings remain missing. Symptom-free days are not assigned a fictional zero score: frequency and intensity answer separate questions.

The display reports average daily peaks and counts of rated feeling days, separately with and without the ingredient. These averages are descriptive summaries, not a claim of equally spaced clinical severity or total symptom burden. An exact conditional label-permutation rank test, including tied ratings, is used for at most 30 rated days. It sums assignments with a doubled mid-rank-sum deviation at least as far from the null expectation as observed. Larger samples use the tie-corrected Mann–Whitney normal approximation with continuity correction; fewer than six ratings in either large-sample group return p=1. Ordinal pairwise superiority counts a higher rating as 1 and a tie as .5. It is an effect description, never a probability of intolerance.

Intensity can generate a finding even when frequency is unchanged. The exploratory display gate is at least three rated feeling days per group, an average peak difference of at least one point and pairwise superiority at least .65. A stronger intensity label additionally requires 14 complete eligible comparison days, at least six complete rated feeling days per group, six confirmed complete exposed rated days, three exposure runs, joint adjusted p <= .05, and the same one-point/.65 effect on complete-only and confirmed-exposure subsets. Existing stress/sleep imbalance screens apply. A weakening of at least .75 points on lower-stress or rested symptom days also prevents the stronger intensity label, with at least three ratings per subgroup. Missing context does not become normal context. These gates are conservative product choices, not clinically validated cutoffs.

Frequency and intensity retain separate statuses and chance checks. Overall readiness uses the stronger eligible signal without labelling an unchanged frequency comparison as significant. Cards, details and reports state whether the clue concerns frequency, intensity, or both. Conditioning on symptom days can introduce selection bias; daily maxima depend on recording habits and do not capture duration, dose or an ongoing symptom's true onset. The existing dependence, missingness and observational limitations still apply. No confidence interval or clinical meaning for severity-score differences is invented.

## Ingredient uncertainty and confounding

Recipes are curated, editable hypotheses. A typed dish never marks its typical ingredients confirmed. Gluten-free variants remove wheat/barley/rye hypotheses and suggest alternative starches as inferred; dairy-free removes milk and lactose; lactose-free preserves milk while removing lactose. Vegan variants remove listed animal ingredients but do not invent a substitute. These rules do not establish allergy safety, cross-contact status, certification, or absence of an ingredient. Actual ingredients and allergen statements always take priority.

Pattern analysis adds gluten as a derived trigger exposure whenever confirmed or inferred wheat, barley or rye is present. This makes the established shared protein group analysable even when the label names only the source cereal. The derived exposure inherits the source confidence, remains linked to the source meal in pattern details, and is never created from gluten-free wording. It does not imply that a diary association diagnoses coeliac disease, wheat allergy or non-coeliac gluten sensitivity. Milk, yoghurt, cream, whey and specified soft dairy also yield a separately marked inferred lactose component, unless an explicit lactose-free claim applies. Amount is not inferred. Butter, ghee, casein and hard cheese do not automatically yield lactose. Milk-protein grouping retains dairy origin without collapsing butter into milk. Explicit lactose entries remain direct exposures. For typed recipes, generic cheese asks for the type, fresh/soft cheese is treated as a lactose hypothesis, and hard/aged cheese is not automatically treated as a meaningful lactose exposure.

The bundled recognition catalogue combines a small curated set with the Open Food Facts ingredient taxonomy pinned to source revision `b0c557fe6b54edf1b2e1ff4d1298ead345bd508a`. Its generated names and synonyms are distributed under the Open Database License; source and generation metadata live with the generated catalogue. Matching uses complete aliases and phrases, normalises known synonyms to one canonical entry (for example, niacin and vitamin B3), resolves regular plural forms against known catalogue entries, and excludes negated/free-from phrases and advisory text. Preparation and appearance variants such as fresh, dried, frozen, chopped or powdered forms resolve through taxonomy parents to a shared curated food family when the relationship is unambiguous. Materially different derivatives and species, such as garlic oil or wild garlic, stay distinct. It does not pull isolated words from unrelated packaging copy. A camera label can therefore be recognised without an explicit Ingredients heading when a run begins with strong catalogue coverage and continues over recognised lines, while uncertain text remains for review instead of becoming an exposure.

Barcode lookup uses the product service's structured ingredient nodes. The product name and ingredient data come from that record; Bellywise does not infer a scanned product's ingredients from its name. The raw published label is retained in the saved entry for audit but the logging UI shows the separated ingredients only. Recognised label ingredients start confirmed; users may unconfirm or remove individual items. Recipe hypotheses remain inferred. Open Food Facts is community-maintained, so records may still be incomplete or inaccurate and the user must review them.

An exposure day is confirmed if any logged meal explicitly confirms that ingredient. The comparison reports how many exposure days depend entirely on inference. Confirmed-only mode excludes an inferred exposure day; it never relabels that day as an unexposed control. Missing ingredients in a recipe can still misclassify absence, so reviewing labels and actual recipes is essential. Unknown food names are preserved with inferred confidence instead of receiving fabricated ingredients. If a manually entered ingredient has no exact catalogue match, the app says so, offers close matches, and requires an explicit choice before storing the original term as a persistent personal ingredient.

Food and drink entries share the same exposure logic. A `kind` field distinguishes them in the journal, defaulting to food for older records. Coffee/tea, alternative milks, juices and alcoholic drinks use editable recipes; decaf and alcohol-free text remove the relevant inferred ingredient. This does not guarantee zero trace caffeine or alcohol. Sugar-free cola retains an unresolved sweetener placeholder until its actual label is reviewed, rather than inventing a particular sweetener. Reviewed raw label text can be retained separately from canonical ingredient names for audit.

Plain water is retained in food and drink records but is excluded from the analysis test family, so it can never be shown as a symptom pattern or as a co-occurring suspect. Other components of a drink, including carbonation, caffeine, alcohol, sugar and sweeteners, remain eligible for comparison.

A Jaccard overlap of at least .8 across at least 3 shared days flags co-ingredients as difficult to separate. The engine also compares high-stress frequency between groups and, with at least 3 exposed and 3 unexposed lower-stress days, reports a lower-stress sensitivity effect. A 30-point difference in high-stress frequency or a 20-point weakening on lower-stress days prevents the stronger label. This is a confounding screen, not a fitted multivariable or causal adjustment. A clinician can decide whether controlled changes or testing are appropriate; the app should never direct allergy re-challenges or unsupervised restrictive diets.

## Local upgrade, September 2026

Whole-food/product hypotheses join the same prespecified test family before multiple-comparison correction. Barcodes identify products; manually named multi-ingredient foods use a bounded stable name identity. Meal groups are organisational only: every item retains its consumption time. Display prefers ingredient patterns and normally omits whole-product patterns. A whole product is an exceptional fallback only when at least two actual food ingredients are present, every component appears exclusively in that product, and the ingredients' exposure records, timing, selected window, outcome and comparison counts are identical with no stronger ingredient evidence. Milk plus derived lactose/protein does not count as several food ingredients. Any independent component observation restores ingredient-first display. The detail list orders background trigger context without claiming an individual cause.

Sleep uses the outcome day's report of the preceding night. With at least three known days in each group, an average sleep difference of 1.5 hours flags imbalance. The engine also compares nights with at least seven recorded hours if there are three exposed and three unexposed observations. A 20-percentage-point weakening flags possible confounding. These heuristic screens prevent the stronger diary label; they do not alter raw rates, fit a causal adjustment or define a personal sleep target. Missing sleep stays missing. Free-text notes do not affect calculations.

Established ingredient background is deliberately separate from statistical evidence. Limited/unknown clinical context lowers the displayed confidence in an intolerance explanation; it does not rewrite p-values or invent a numerical prior. Ingredient-level information is not a validated food-by-symptom likelihood model.

The 26-week food-group view counts recorded presence on distinct days and shows diary coverage. It does not estimate servings, nutrients, deficiencies, or food-group-to-symptom causation. Oils/flavours/extracts are not counted as servings of their source group.

Photo region selection uses normalized Vision line bounds (converted from bottom-left to top-left coordinates), retaining lines at least 60% inside the selected rectangle. It selects recognised lines rather than generating a cropped image, and users should include complete lines. Parsing still applies whole-phrase matching and packaging exclusions after selection.
